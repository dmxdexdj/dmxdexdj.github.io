A tiny glossary

Packet
A small chunk of data. Big things like photos are split into lots of packets, sent separately and put back together at the end.
IP address
A number that says where a device is, like 192.168.1.20 on a home network. Every device online has one.
DNS
The internet’s phone book. It turns a name like example.com into an IP address your device can use.
Router
A box that reads where a packet is going and passes it on to the next router along the way.
NAT
A router trick that lets many devices share one public address. The router keeps a table of who asked for what.
CGNAT
NAT done by your ISP across lots of homes at once. Cheaper for them, but you lose the ability to accept incoming connections.
Hop
One jump from one router to the next. The more hops, the longer the trip usually takes.
Latency
How long data takes to arrive, measured in milliseconds (ms). Lower is better, especially for games.

Packets from the same file can take different routes and arrive out of order. Your device puts them back together.

Why your devices have fake addresses

There are only about 4.3 billion IPv4 addresses, and far more devices than that. So your home shares one public address, and every device inside gets a private one like 192.168.1.10. Your router does the swapping. That trick is called NAT, short for network address translation.

Some ISPs have run out of addresses too. They put another NAT, called CGNAT (carrier-grade NAT), in front of whole neighbourhoods. Now your router doesn’t even get a public address. It gets a shared one starting with 100.64, and many homes use the same public address at once. Try both below.

      What just happened

        Why CGNAT is such a pain

        You can’t host anything
        Game servers, cameras and remote desktop all need someone outside to reach in. Behind CGNAT there’s no way in, because the ISP’s box drops anything nobody asked for.
        Port forwarding stops working
        You can set the rule on your router perfectly and it still won’t help. The packet is dropped one step earlier, at a box you don’t control.
        Peer-to-peer gets slower
        Voice calls and games often can’t connect directly, so they send everything through a relay server instead. That adds lag.
        Strangers share your address
        Many homes can look like one IP address, so a ban or block aimed at one person can hit everyone using it.
        Consoles complain
        Stack another NAT on top of yours and consoles report a strict NAT type, which limits who you can play with.
        Problems are hard to trace
        Tools and logs show the shared address, not you, so working out what went wrong often needs your ISP’s help.

        Ways around it

        Ask your ISP for a public IPv4 address (some charge a small fee). Use IPv6, which has so many addresses that NAT isn’t needed. Or rent a small server somewhere and tunnel your traffic through it.

        Packet Park is a toy. Real networks have millions of routers, and the times here are made up.
